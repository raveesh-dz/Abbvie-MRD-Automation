# Takeaways — run_2026-06-26_001
Crohn's new-prescribing Holdout analysis · STELARA → SKYRIZI · NBRx · last 13 weeks (w/e 2026-06-05)

1. **SKYRIZI already wins Crohn's new prescribing — STELARA is a thin minority.** Across all 1,040 HCPs, SKYRIZI drew 666 CD NBRx over the last 13 weeks versus 49 for STELARA (≈13.6× more new SKYRIZI starts). Among the 413 HCPs who wrote any CD NBRx, 370 (90%) are Non-Holdouts where SKYRIZI carries their new prescribing — only 43 still lean on STELARA. The brand switch is largely complete where new Crohn's prescribing is actually happening.

2. **670 Holdouts (64%) — but the headline overstates the conversion target.** Of the 670 Holdouts, 627 (60% of all HCPs) wrote NO CD NBRx at all in the window (group `no_cd_nbrx`). They are Holdouts by definition (no SKYRIZI), but there is no recent STELARA habit to switch — they are an activation / opportunity-sizing question, not a STELARA-to-SKYRIZI conversion. Treating all 670 as one target list would point most of the effort at silent prescribers.

3. **The real switch list is 43 active STELARA-leaners.** These are the HCPs writing new Crohn's scripts where STELARA still holds at least half of SKYRIZI's share: 26 write STELARA only with zero SKYRIZI (`stelara_only`), and 17 write both but lean STELARA (`both_lean_stelara`, ratio ≥ 0.5). This is the concentrated, high-yield conversion target — doctors who are actively starting Crohn's patients but defaulting to STELARA over SKYRIZI.

4. **Non-Holdouts (370, 36%) are a retention play, and they are nearly all decisive.** 367 are SKYRIZI-only (`skyrizi_only`, zero STELARA) and just 3 write both while still favouring SKYRIZI (`both_lean_skyrizi`, ratio < 0.5). With almost no "both-brand, SKYRIZI-leaning" middle, the converted base shows little active STELARA competition for their new starts — the priority for this group is holding share, not winning it.
